The tax bill you do not see on one form

Your paycheck is not the whole tax base.

A working household pays taxes directly, through payroll, through prices, through property, and through taxes legally charged to employers and businesses but economically passed to workers and consumers. This model puts those channels on one ledger without counting the same tax twice.

Benchmark family

Married filing jointly · two qualifying children

Default income is the 2024 median married-couple family income. State/local default is ITEP’s U.S. average for its fourth income quintile, which spans $80,400–$138,300 and excludes seniors.

Hidden incidence

Who writes the check is not always who bears the cost.

CBO treats the employer share of payroll tax as borne by workers through lower compensation, allocates corporate income tax partly to labor and capital owners, and allocates excise taxes according to household consumption.

Employer payroll tax

The employer remits another 6.2% Social Security tax up to each worker’s wage cap plus 1.45% Medicare. CBO’s incidence method treats employer payroll taxes as part of workers’ economic tax burden. The model also adds net FUTA at 0.6% of the first $7,000 per worker, assuming the standard full state credit.

Business taxes in prices

ITEP’s state/local model includes business sales and excise taxes, corporate income taxes, and other business/property taxes that are not itemized on a household receipt.

Customs duties

Tariffs are taxes collected at the border. The Budget Lab’s current-law estimate puts their ultimate consumer cost at roughly $1,100 per household per year.

State & local anatomy

The 10.3% is already broad.

ITEP’s fourth-quintile U.S. average covers 99.7% of state and local tax revenue and explicitly excludes senior households, making it unusually well matched to the working-family benchmark.

Method & limits

“True” means incidence-aware, not magically exact.

There is no single observable receipt for taxes passed through wages and prices. The model therefore combines current statutory taxes with established incidence estimates and labels each assumption.

2026 federal income tax

Married-filing-jointly 2026 brackets, $32,200 standard deduction, and modeled $2,200 Child Tax Credit per qualifying child, subject to the usual eligibility rules.

Federal hidden taxes

Employer payroll is calculated under 2026 Social Security/Medicare law. Federal excise uses CBO’s 0.6% middle-quintile effective rate. Federal corporate incidence uses a conservative 0.8% fourth-quintile CBO/CRS benchmark.

No double counting

The state/local 10.3% already includes state corporate taxes, business sales/excise, and other state/local taxes. Those are not added again. Tariffs are separate federal customs duties.

Denominator check: the headline rate divides all modeled taxes by the salary a worker sees. A second rate divides by salary plus employer FICA. Both are shown because using only one denominator can make the same burden look artificially larger or smaller.
