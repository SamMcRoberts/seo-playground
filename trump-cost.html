What has Trump policy added to a working family’s annual burden?

The answer is not one magic number. Some costs can be priced cleanly at the household level; others are real but overlap with those costs or do not have a defensible household-dollar conversion. This tracker adds only what can be added without double counting, then shows the wider exposure separately.

Defaults: BLS 2024 average consumer-unit spending; two qualifying children; Budget Lab Aug. 24, 2026 current-law tariff estimate; and a rough annualized $700B/15-year Energy Innovation consumer-cost exposure using today’s household count. The energy number is shown as a sensitivity because some tariff/inflation effects are already embedded in that model’s inputs.

The wider burden

Real channels that should be counted conceptually, but not stacked into a fake precise total.

Adding GDP loss, hiring loss, price effects, and uncertainty on top of one another would count the same economic damage several times. They belong in the ledger, but not all in the arithmetic sum.

Energy, electricity & cancelled renewables

Energy Innovation models seven policy groups since January 2025, including OBBBA energy provisions, power-plant rule changes, repeal of tailpipe rules, limits on wind permitting, hydrogen-hub funding cancellations, and cancellation of the $7B Solar for All program. It estimates $650B in extra household energy costs through 2040, plus $43B in healthcare costs.

Fuel

The same model estimates federal transportation-policy reversals raise average gasoline prices 14% in 2035 and 26% in 2040. It explicitly distinguishes those policy effects from near-term Iran-war and other market pressures, so this tracker does not lazily charge every gasoline spike to domestic policy.

Jobs & GDP

Energy Innovation estimates these federal energy-policy changes reduce U.S. GDP by $2.3T cumulatively through 2040 and cost 820,000 jobs per year on average over the next decade. Those losses matter to households, but converting the aggregate directly into “$X per household” would double-count and misallocate incidence.

Trade-policy uncertainty

Atlanta Fed survey evidence found policy uncertainty, dominated by tariffs, led firms to plan roughly 13% less hiring and 16% less investment over the following six months. This is a household burden through weaker labor demand and growth, but no credible source gives a clean per-household dollar incidence.

Household caution

A 2026 Federal Reserve household study finds tariff-exposed retail prices rose while purchases fell much more; aggregate spending on affected goods fell about three times as much as prices rose, with much of the contraction tied to pessimism and precautionary behavior.

Trade agreements & retaliation

Changes in China tariff rates, the U.K. deal, auto rebates, exemptions, retaliation, court rulings, and replacement tariff authorities are already reflected in the dated Budget Lab tariff snapshots. They are not added a second time as a separate “deal uncertainty” tax.

What is in the energy model

The “cancelled renewables” burden is not hand-waving.

The July 2026 Energy Innovation model specifically enumerates policy changes and runs them through its Energy Policy Simulator. Those effects are forward-looking, not a claim that every dollar has already hit a 2026 utility bill.

Method

A floor, a broader exposure, and no bullshit precision.

The tracker intentionally separates additive household-dollar estimates from overlapping macroeconomic channels.

1. Current priced burden

Current-law tariff burden + 0.3% of household spending from the IMF’s estimated end-2026 consumer-price effect of reduced immigration − $200 per qualifying child in incremental Child Tax Credit relief versus the pre-2025 $2,000 credit.

2. Broader annualized exposure

Adds a rough $340/year household annualization of Energy Innovation’s rounded $700B combined energy + healthcare consumer cost over 15 years. It is displayed separately because the report says some tariff and inflation pressure is already embedded in source inputs.

3. Unpriced but real

Employment, GDP, investment, trade-policy uncertainty, precautionary behavior, and market volatility are disclosed with source estimates but not monetized into the household total absent a defensible incidence model.

Why the tax offset is only $200 per child: the benchmark is lived conditions at the start of 2025. The 2025 law raised the maximum CTC from $2,000 to $2,200. Treating the full extension of pre-existing TCJA rates as a new household windfall would mix a statutory-expiration scoring baseline with a lived-experience baseline.
